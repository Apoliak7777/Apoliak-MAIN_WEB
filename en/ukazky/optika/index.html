02

Your visit

What an eye test involves

Five steps, 30 minutes in all. During the test the examination room is reserved for one person.

  1. Step 1

    A quick chat first

    We go over how you see now, what bothers you, the work you do and the glasses you wear. We read the prescription of your current glasses on a focimeter.

    5 minutes
  2. Step 2

    Objective measurement

    An autorefractor measures the starting values for both eyes, including the curvature of the cornea. It's contact-free and doesn't hurt.

    5 minutes
  3. Step 3

    Subjective refraction

    In a trial frame we compare lenses one after another at a test distance of five metres. This is where the prescription for your glasses is decided.

    10 minutes
  4. Step 4

    Near vision and how your eyes work together

    We fine-tune the reading addition and check binocular balance between your eyes. That tells us whether an office lens or a varifocal makes sense.

    5 minutes
  5. Step 5

    Recommendation and choice

    We explain the result and recommend the lens type and thinning to suit your prescription and frame. You take the written record with you even if you don't order glasses from us.

    5 minutes

What to bring

  • The glasses you wear now, including reading glasses
  • Your last eye test record, if you have one
  • A list of any long-term medication you take
  • Take out contact lenses at least two hours before the test
  • Children under fifteen come with a parent

An eye test at the opticians doesn't replace an examination by an eye doctor. If we find something that needs a doctor, we'll tell you and recommend an examination.

Beyond everyday glasses

Specialist care

Three areas, each with its own process, price and timescale. The eye test before them works the same as for everyday glasses: €20 and 30 minutes.

Specialism 01

Myopia control for children

Short-sightedness usually gets worse during the school years. We fit spectacle lenses with peripheral defocus or daily multifocal contact lenses, and check the prescription every six months. We record every result on a card, so after a year you can see the whole progression in black and white.

Lenses from €249 a pair · check-ups included

Book a test for your child
Specialism 02

Keratoconus

With keratoconus the cornea changes shape and glasses are no longer enough. We fit rigid gas-permeable and scleral lenses, but only on the basis of an eye doctor's report no more than six months old. The fitting takes 60 minutes; you wear a trial pair for two weeks, and only then do we order your own lens.

Fitting €60 · lens from €120 each · delivery 3 – 4 weeks

Book a fitting
Specialism 03

Rigid and overnight lenses

Overnight orthokeratology lenses are worn while you sleep, and during the day you see without glasses. We fit them up to -4.50 dioptres and up to 2.00 cylinder. Check-ups after the first night, after a week and after a month are included in the fitting. If they don't suit you within 30 days of collection, we refund the cost of the lenses.

04

Choosing by use

Types of spectacle lenses

The lens type decides at which distances you'll see sharply through your glasses. We recommend one based on your test results and what you do during the day.

Type 01

Single vision

One prescription across the whole lens, either for distance or for reading. The easiest to get used to, and suitable for first glasses and for children.

From €39 a pair

Type 02

Office lenses (degressive)

Sharp vision from your desk to about four metres. A wide field for the screen and paper, but not suitable for driving. They work well as a second pair alongside varifocals.

Screen and desk · €129

Type 03

Varifocals

A smooth transition from distance through intermediate to reading in one lens, with no visible dividing line. We offer three levels, depending on the width of the comfortable field of vision.

From age 45 · €189 – 359

Type 04

Prescription sunglasses

A lens with your prescription, tinted or polarised. Polarisation removes glare from the road, water and snow, so we recommend it for drivers.

Tint €20 · polarisation €55

Type 05

Photochromic

A self-tinting lens that darkens in daylight and clears indoors. Behind a car windscreen it only darkens partly — keep that in mind.

Extra €65 a pair

Type 06

Thinned lenses

A higher index means a thinner, lighter lens for the same prescription. We always recommend it over four dioptres, and sooner for rimless glasses.

Index 1.6 to 1.74 · €30 – 140

How to choose

  • You work at a screen more than four hours a day — office lenses
  • Switching between two pairs bothers you — varifocals
  • You're over four dioptres and the thickness bothers you — a higher index
  • You drive in the dark and glare bothers you — an anti-reflective coating
  • You're outdoors a lot — photochromic or prescription sunglasses

What every lens includes

After collection

Looking after your glasses

Scratches on lenses mostly come from wiping them dry, and frames go out of shape from putting them on with one hand.

Daily

Cleaning your lenses

Rinse with lukewarm water, add a drop of washing-up liquid, rinse again and dry with a clean microfibre cloth. Wiping them dry rubs dust into the coating and scratches it.

Cloth included with your glasses

Daily

Putting them away

In the case, never lenses down on the table. In summer don't leave your glasses on the car dashboard — temperatures over 60 °C damage the anti-reflective coating and the shape of the frame.

Case included with your glasses

Daily

Putting them on

Always with both hands. Putting them on with one hand stretches one arm, the glasses tilt, and varifocal lenses then don't sit at the right height.

Adjustments any time, free of charge

What to avoid

  • Wiping with a T-shirt, a napkin or a paper tissue
  • Alcohol and window cleaners — they break down the anti-reflective coating

Your questions

FAQ

The eight questions we're asked most often in store. If your answer isn't here, give us a call or write to us through the form.

Do I need to book an eye test?

We recommend it, because the test takes 30 minutes and the examination room is reserved for one person during that time. Without an appointment we only test when there happens to be a free slot. You can try frames on any time during opening hours, though.

How long does it take to make glasses?

The usual time is 3 to 5 working days, and 7 working days for individual varifocal lenses. Express within 24 hours costs €25 and applies to single vision and office lenses; we can't do varifocals or myopia control lenses that fast.

Can I have new lenses put in my own frame?

Yes — fitting to your own frame costs €15. We check the frame first: with old or brittle acetate there's a risk of it cracking during glazing, and we'll warn you about that in advance. If you don't accept the risk, we won't start the work.

What if I can't get used to varifocals?

There's a 60-day adaptation guarantee on varifocal lenses. Within two months of collection we'll swap them for another type — office lenses, say, or two pairs of single vision glasses. You only pay the difference in price; the extra work is free.

Do you test children's eyes?

We test children from age six, always with a parent present. If the prescription is getting worse, we offer myopia control lenses with a check-up every six months. If we suspect a lazy eye, a squint or anything else, we don't take the child on and recommend an examination by an eye doctor.

What's the difference between index 1.5 and 1.67?

The index determines how strongly the lens bends light, and so how thick it is. For the same prescription, a 1.67 lens is much thinner and lighter than a 1.5. Up to four dioptres the difference is small; over four dioptres we recommend thinning, especially for rimless glasses.

Will my health insurance pay for my glasses?

We don't bill health insurers directly, so you won't get the refund through us. But for every order we issue a receipt with the date, prescription, lens type and price, and you claim the refund from your insurer with it. We can also issue the receipt later, up to 24 months after collection.

What should I clean my glasses with?

Lukewarm water with a drop of washing-up liquid, then a microfibre cloth. Alcohol, window cleaners and wiping them dry with a T-shirt damage the anti-reflective coating. Bring your glasses in every three months — we do ultrasonic cleaning while you wait, free of charge.
