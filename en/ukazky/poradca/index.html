How we work

Four rules we stick to

Financial advice has a bad reputation in Ireland, and we know why. These are the ways we try to be different.

First we ask, then we suggest

The first meeting is all about you — what you have, what you pay and what you're planning. We don't offer you anything at it. A proposal only comes at the second meeting, once there's something to propose.

no sales pitch at the first meeting

We tell you when nothing needs doing

About a third of clients leave the first meeting without changing anything. Their finances are set up well, and it would make no sense to redo them just so we could earn something.

a third change nothing

We talk about the risks too

Every product has another side — charges, lock-in periods, falls in value. If someone only shows you a graph going up, that's not advice, it's selling.

both sides

We stay in touch

Once a year we check whether anything has changed — income, family, property prices. A policy from 2015 often doesn't fit today's situation, and without a review you wouldn't know.

once a year

How it works

FAQ

What you ask us most often

How much will it cost me?

The consultation, the policy review and the annual review are free. When we arrange something, we're paid by the institution whose product you take out — for insurance and mortgages that doesn't change your price. Investments have an entry charge, and we always show it to you in euro, not as a percentage hidden in a table.

Are you really independent?

We work with nine institutions, not one. That means we can compare, but it doesn't mean we're neutral — commission varies between products, and that's a conflict of interest every intermediary has. So we'd rather name it, and we'll tell you the commission if you ask.

Do you guarantee returns?

No, and nobody is allowed to guarantee them. The figures in the calculator are a model with constant growth, so you can see the principle. In reality returns fluctuate — with an adventurous portfolio, a fall of a third during a crisis is common, even when the long-term average looks good.

I have old policies from 2010 — is it worth cancelling them?

Usually not straight away. Older policies sometimes have terms you can't buy today — a guaranteed rate, for example, or illness cover with no waiting period. Cancelling them and taking out new ones tends to benefit the intermediary, not the client. So we read them first and only then say anything.

What if the insurer won't pay out?

We help with the paperwork and the appeal, free of charge for our clients. Payouts are most often reduced because of underinsurance or an unreported change — both can be avoided with an annual review, which is why we insist on it.

Do I have to sign anything straight away?

No. And if someone pressures you by saying an offer is only valid today, that's a reason to walk away. Our proposals stand until market rates change, and when they do, we'll get in touch and recalculate.
