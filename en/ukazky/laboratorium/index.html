Accreditation

What it means that we're accredited

A company can't accredit itself — accreditation is granted by the Irish National Accreditation Board (INAB), which checks it every year with a surveillance audit.

ISO/IEC 17025

The standard for the competence of testing and calibration laboratories. It checks the method, the instruments, the competence of the people and the independence of the result.

since 2005

Traceability to standards

Every instrument we use is calibrated against one that is calibrated to a national standard. Without that chain, a measured value is just a number.

documented

Measurement uncertainty

Our reports give the measured value and the uncertainty. Without it you can't say whether a result really passed — and that's the part non-accredited measurements leave out.

always stated

Recognition of reports

Accredited reports are recognised by building control authorities, inspectors, insurers and courts. In a dispute, the other side will challenge a non-accredited measurement.

How it works

Five steps from sample to report

You tell us what needs checking

If you don't know the standard, we'll tell you. The advice is free and saves the most time in the whole process.

You bring the sample, or we come to you

For some tests the laboratory has to take the sample itself — otherwise the report wouldn't be accredited. We'll tell you in advance.

We receive and register it

The sample gets a number and is traceable from then on. You get a receipt confirmation with the date.

We carry out the test

To the standard, on calibrated instruments and by qualified staff. With concrete we wait for it to cure — 28 days can't be shortened.

We issue the report

Within five working days of the test, or 48 hours express. In two copies and electronically; we archive it for ten years.

FAQ

What you ask us most often

What's the difference between an accredited and a non-accredited measurement?

An accredited measurement has a validated method, calibrated instruments traceable to national standards and a stated measurement uncertainty. A non-accredited one gives you a number — an accredited one gives you a number that stands up before an authority and in court. The price difference is usually smaller than people expect.

Can I take the concrete sample myself?

You can, and it's common, but the cubes have to be made and cured to the standard — the right mould, compaction, and storage in water at 20 °C. If that isn't followed, the result tells you about the sampling, not the concrete. In disputed cases we take the samples ourselves, and then we stand behind the sampling.

Why do you wait 28 days for concrete strength?

Because the standard strength is defined after 28 days of curing — until then the concrete is still gaining strength. We can do a 7-day test as a guide, but the report for building control sign-off needs the 28-day result. There's no way round this, not even for a surcharge.

How long does it take to issue a report?

Within five working days of the test, or 48 hours express for a surcharge. For concrete, add the curing time. We confirm the date when we receive the sample, and we stick to it.

What if the sample fails?

We write it in the report exactly as it is — including the measured value and the uncertainty. We don't round in the client's favour and we don't issue “corrected” reports. We'll advise you on the next options: an additional test, cores, or a structural assessment of the actual condition.

Do you also do tests you're not accredited for?

Yes, but we always state it explicitly in the report. There are cases where accreditation isn't needed — a guide measurement for your own use, a check before an official test. Passing off a non-accredited measurement as accredited would be deception, and we don't do that.
