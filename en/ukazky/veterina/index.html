01 / CLINIC

About the clinic

The clinic and what we cover

The clinic has three consulting rooms, a dedicated operating theatre, an X-ray and ultrasound room, its own laboratory and a hospital ward with separate areas for dogs and cats. Examination, imaging and surgery can all happen on the same day, with no need to move your pet elsewhere.

Scope

Our patients

Dogs, cats, rabbits and small rodents, pet birds and reptiles. We don't treat farm animals or horses.

Appointments

Booked for an exact time

Appointments are booked in 45-minute slots. A routine examination takes 30 minutes, leaving time for questions and notes.

Records

Patient record

Every procedure, result and vaccination goes on the record. We can issue a copy or a report for another practice within 2 working days.

The daily routine

Mornings and afternoons

Surgery in the mornings, consultations and preventive care in the afternoons. The ward has 6 kennels and a separate isolation kennel.

06 / PREPARATION

Before surgery

Preparing your pet for a general anaesthetic

These instructions are for planned procedures under general anaesthetic. For emergency admissions, we go by your pet's condition and give you instructions by phone.

24 hours before

The day before

  • Dogs and cats: last meal by 20:00 the evening before.
  • Water stays freely available until the morning.
  • Rabbits, rodents, birds and reptiles should not be starved at all — leave food in the cage.
  • Make a list of any medication your pet takes, with the doses.
  • Brush long-haired breeds — it makes preparing the surgical site easier.
Admission 7:45

On the morning of surgery

  • Don't give any food or treats in the morning.
  • Take water away 2 hours before you arrive.
  • Take your dog for a short walk so it can go to the toilet.
  • Bring the passport or vaccination card, and your pet insurance details if you have them.
  • Bring a blanket or towel from home for the kennel.
The first 48 hours

After you get home

  • On the first evening, give only a third of the normal meal; water as much as they like.
  • 24 hours' rest: no stairs, no jumping and no running free outside.
  • Keep the cone or body suit on as instructed.
  • Check the wound twice a day — it shouldn't be weeping.
  • A check-up after 3 days and stitch removal after 10 to 14 days, both included in the price.

When to call straight away

If your pet hasn't drunk anything for 12 hours after surgery, is vomiting repeatedly, the wound is bleeding or visibly open, or the breathing seems fast and laboured, call us. We'll advise you what to do and, if necessary, see you outside consulting hours.

Come straight away — don't wait until morning

  • An injury, a fall from a height or being hit by a car, even if your pet seems calm.
  • Laboured or rapid breathing, bluish gums, collapse.
  • A swollen, hard belly with unsuccessful attempts to vomit.
  • Seizures lasting more than two minutes, or one after another.
  • A male cat repeatedly trying to pee and passing nothing.
  • Suspected poisoning, a sting, or swallowing a foreign object.
  • Heavy bleeding that doesn't stop after five minutes of pressure.
  • A birth that goes on for more than an hour without progress.
  • Overheating in a car or in the sun, temperature over 40 degrees.

What to do on the way to the clinic

  • Call before you set off. Tell us the species, weight, age and what happened.
  • Don't give human medicines. Common painkillers are poisonous to dogs and cats.
  • Don't make your pet vomit unless the vet tells you to — with corrosive substances it causes more damage.
  • Carry an animal that can't move on something rigid, such as a board or a sturdy carrier.
  • Press on the bleeding with a clean cloth, and keep the pressure on during the journey.
  • Bring the packaging or what's left of the substance — with poisoning, it's the fastest route to the right antidote.
  • Cool an overheated animal with lukewarm water, never ice-cold, and bring it to the clinic straight away.

What to expect when you arrive

  • Emergency treatment is paid on the spot, in cash or by card.
  • Appointments the next day may be moved because of an emergency; we'll let you know.
  • If your pet is hospitalised, you'll get an update by phone every morning.

17 / QUESTIONS

FAQ

FAQ

The nine questions we're asked most at reception, with prices, timescales and conditions.

Do I need an appointment, or can I just come in?
Booked patients come first, and we can set aside a whole slot for them. Without an appointment, we'll see you if a slot frees up; expect to wait, and a €10 surcharge if another appointment has to be moved. Emergencies are always seen, without this surcharge.
Which animals do you treat?
Dogs, cats, rabbits, guinea pigs, rats, hamsters, pet birds and reptiles. We don't treat farm animals, horses or bees, and we don't have the equipment for them.
How long does a routine visit take?
Appointments are booked in 45-minute slots, and the examination itself takes 20 to 30 minutes. If a blood test or imaging is needed, allow an hour to an hour and a half, including waiting for the results.
When do I get lab results?
We process blood counts, biochemistry and urine here at the clinic, with results within 20 minutes while you're still here. Samples sent to an external lab, such as cytology, take 3 to 5 working days; we'll call you.
Can I stay with my pet during treatment?
For examinations and routine treatment, yes — it actually helps. Not for procedures under anaesthetic or X-rays, for hygiene and safety reasons. When you drop off and collect your pet, we explain in detail what happened.
What about microchipping and passports?
Microchipping with registration costs €28 and takes about 20 minutes; in Ireland it's compulsory for all dogs. We issue an EU pet passport for €18, which requires a valid microchip and a valid rabies vaccination. For travel abroad, sort out the passport at least 21 days before you leave, because the vaccination has to take effect.
Why do you take blood before surgery?
A pre-op blood test shows how the liver and kidneys, which process the anaesthetic, are working, and picks up hidden inflammation or anaemia. Depending on the result, we adjust the dose or postpone the procedure. We take the sample on the morning of the procedure and have the result within 20 minutes.
What if I can't make my appointment?
Let us know at least 12 hours before, and we'll move it free of charge. For later cancellations or not turning up for a surgery appointment, we charge €15, because we can no longer give the slot to another patient.
How quickly do you reply to messages from the form?
Your message goes to the clinic inbox and we reply within 24 hours. The form isn't suitable for emergencies — then call your number here, which is also answered outside consulting hours.
