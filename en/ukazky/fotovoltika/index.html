Grant

How it works and where it most often goes wrong

Grant schemes change, but the rules tend to stay similar. These four things decide whether you get the grant and whether it's actually paid out.

Application first, then installation

This is the most common mistake. If you have the panels fitted before your grant is approved, you lose the grant — costs from before approval don't count.

the order matters

The grant offer expires

It's usually valid for eight months. If the installation and the claim aren't done by then, it lapses. So we plan the dates so it works out even if the supplier runs late.

we keep track for you

The installer has to be registered

Not every company is on the SEAI list of registered solar PV companies. If it isn't, your grant won't be paid — even if the installation is done properly. Check before you sign.

we're on the list

The grant doesn't cover everything

It's capped according to system size and doesn't apply to every part of the setup. So work out the full price and treat the grant as a discount, not as half the price.

capped per kWp

FAQ

What you ask us most often

Is solar PV worth it without a battery?

It depends on when you use electricity. If you're out during the day, you use about a third of your output and export the rest to the grid for a fraction of the price. That's when a battery makes sense. If you have a heat pump or a pool, or you work from home, it can work out well without one.

How long do panels last?

Manufacturers give a 25-year output warranty with a guaranteed drop of around 0.5% a year. The panels are the most reliable part of the system. The first thing to fail is usually the inverter — expect to replace it after twelve to fifteen years; it costs around €1,200.

What about winter and cloudy days?

It still generates, just less — in December about a tenth of what it does in June. That's why a system isn't designed to cover winter; that would mean a huge surplus in summer. The aim is to cover the annual balance, not every single day.

Will the installation damage my roof?

Not with proper fixings. We use hooks under the tiles or screws into the structure with special seals, and we give a ten-year guarantee against leaks on our work. If your roof is near the end of its life, we'll honestly recommend replacing it first.

Do I have to notify ESB Networks?

Yes — every system connected to the grid has to be notified and needs a completion certificate. We handle it all, including the connection paperwork. Without notification you risk being disconnected, and you can't be paid for exported surplus.

Do you also do off-grid systems for holiday homes?

Yes, for buildings without a grid connection. They're different setups — with a bigger battery and no link to the grid, so the grant doesn't apply. The payback isn't measured against your bills there, but against the cost of a grid connection, which can run to tens of thousands.
